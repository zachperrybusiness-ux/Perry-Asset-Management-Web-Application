About Perry Asset Management

A disciplined, research-driven approach to portfolio management — built on macro intelligence, quantitative rigor, and institutional-grade analytics.

Our Approach

Perry Asset Management combines top-down macro regime analysis with bottom-up fundamental and quantitative research. Our process begins with a six-pillar macro scorecard built on 24 FRED series covering Growth, Labor Market, Inflation, Monetary Policy, Fiscal Policy, and Global Liquidity. Each indicator carries an explicit threshold, a published release lag, and a dated observation, so nothing is presented as more current than it is.

Those pillars feed a single unified signal engine that resolves four distinct read-outs — macro regime, market phase, price trend, and your own risk mandate — into one stated portfolio posture. Where signals disagree, the site shows the conflict and the horizon each operates on rather than silently picking a winner.

📚 Research Context — Hedgefundie / Academic Background

The Volatility Decay Equation

A leveraged ETF with multiplier L and underlying daily volatility σ experiences Decay ≈ L(L−1)/2 × σ² per day. At 2% daily vol: 2× loses ~0.02%/day (≈5%/yr), 3× loses ~0.12%/day (≈30%/yr) to pure compounding drag — independent of market direction.

Source: Avellaneda & Zhang (2010)

The 200-SMA + VIX Filter

Academic and practitioner research (Quantpedia, Bogleheads HFEA thread) shows that holding 3× leverage only when the underlying is above its 200-day SMA AND VIX is below ~25–30 dramatically improves Sharpe and cuts max drawdown. The filter alone roughly halves expected max drawdown vs. buy-and-hold.

Source: Quantpedia "Volatility Edge" series • Bogleheads HFEA thread (2019–2026)

⚠ Risk Disclosure: Leveraged ETFs are designed for short-term use. A 3× ETF on a 2000-dotcom equivalent drawdown would lose ~99.8% of value. The HFEA strategy (55% UPRO / 45% TMF) was down 65%+ in 2022 when both equities and bonds fell simultaneously. This tool is for education and risk awareness — not a recommendation.

Walk-Forward Backtest (Out-of-Sample Validation) ?

Tests the MLR model's out-of-sample performance by training on an expanding window and testing on the next period, repeatedly. Standard backtests suffer from in-sample overfitting — a model that looks great when evaluated on the data it was trained on may perform poorly on new data. Walk-forward estimates realistic forward performance by never testing on data the model has seen.

Enter a ticker and click "Run Walk-Forward Test" to see out-of-sample predictive performance.
Method: Expanding-window walk-forward validation (White 2000, Pesaran & Timmermann 1995) • Features: 21-day momentum, 63-day volatility, 10-day mean reversion, trailing return, VIX level, beta, RSI14 proxy • OLS regression trained on history up to each fold boundary • Strategy rule: enter long when model's predicted 21-day return exceeds threshold, hold for prediction horizon, exit • Compared against buy-and-hold baseline • No look-ahead bias — every prediction uses only data available at that point in time.

Why might this differ from the Macro Regime Analysis page?
The Portfolio State here (Leveraged / Growth / Neutral / Drawdown) is a market-timing signal driven by VIX level, SPY distance from its 52-week high, and short-term price momentum. It answers: "What posture should my portfolio take right now based on current market conditions?"
The Macro Regime Verdict on the Macro page (Early / Mid / Late Cycle / Contraction) is a business cycle classification driven by FRED economic indicators (yield curve, unemployment, leading indicators, etc.). It answers: "Where are we in the economic cycle?"
These frameworks operate at different frequencies and measure different things. Omega-Ratio Regime-Conditioned Rebalancing Signal ?

The Omega ratio Ω(τ) = E[max(R−τ,0)] / E[max(τ−R,0)] measures full-distribution risk-adjusted return above a threshold τ. Ω > 1 means expected upside above τ exceeds expected downside below τ. Combined with the Perry regime state, this signals when rebalancing toward or away from risk assets is supported by the full return distribution — not just mean/variance. Particularly useful for validating tactical tilts suggested by the Master Verdict.

SPY, TLT, GLD, IWM, QQQ, HYG · 63-day rolling
Framework: Lyu, B. et al. (2023) "Omega: Robust Online Portfolio Optimization with Cash Flows." Ω(τ) = ∫_τ^∞(1−F(r))dr / ∫_{−∞}^τ F(r)dr computed from empirical return distribution (63-day rolling). τ = daily return threshold. Ω > 1.5 = strong upside skew (add exposure); Ω < 0.8 = downside risk dominant (reduce exposure). Regime-conditioned: threshold adjusted by 0.5× in Drawdown regime (more conservative) and 1.5× in Leveraged regime (more aggressive upside expected). · Price data via Cloudflare Worker / Yahoo Finance.

Cross-Asset Regime Distance & RMT Correlation Analysis ?

Wasserstein-1 distance (Horvath et al. 2021) between rolling return distributions and stylized historical regimes. Unlike Frobenius distance on correlations, Wasserstein measures the full distributional shift — capturing changes in tail behavior, skewness, and kurtosis that correlation matrices miss. Also computes RMT Informative Content Ratio (Molero-González et al. 2024): fraction of eigenvalue variance above the Marchenko-Pastur noise ceiling — a direct measure of how much genuine cross-asset structure exists vs. random noise.

Click Compute Regime Distance + RMT to run analysis.
Methods: Wasserstein-1: W₁(P,Q) = ∫|F_P(x) − F_Q(x)|dx — earth-mover distance between empirical CDFs of rolling returns. Measures distributional shift, not just correlation structure. Horvath, B., Issa, A. & Muguruza, A. (2021) "Clustering Market Regimes using the Wasserstein Distance." · Frobenius: ‖C_t − C_ref‖_F shown for comparison. · RMT / Marchenko-Pastur: λ_max = σ²(1 + 1/√q)² where q = T/N. Eigenvalues above λ_max are informative; below are noise. ICR = Σ(λ_i − λ̄_noise)·1[λ_i > λ_max] / Σλ_i. Molero-González, L. et al. (2024) "The random matrix-based informative content of correlation matrices in stock markets." · Universe: SPY, TLT, GLD, UUP, USO, HYG. Price data via Cloudflare Worker / Yahoo Finance.
